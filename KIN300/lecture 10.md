##### Metabolism
*metabolism:* the series of chemical in living organisms that create and break down energy necessary for life
*metabolic rate:* the rate at which your body expends energy or burns calories

the two classes of chemical processes that make up metabolism is *anabolism* and *catabolism*
##### anabolism - "growth"
set of metabolic reactions that require energy to synthesize (build) new molecules from simpler resources.

food intake sparks anabolism through biosynthetic pathways

the *building* process in our body, requires energy (ATP) for it to occur
- building muscle
- healing a cut
- growing stronger bones
- storing energy
##### catabolism - "destruction"
set of metabolic reactions that transforms fuel into (cellular) energy
- examples: 
	- Glycogenolysis - breakdown of glycogen to  glucose
	- Glycolysis - the breakdown of glucose/glycogen to pyruvate

this *releases energy* (ATP)

**Extracting Energy From Food**
1 *digestion of energy-yielding nutrients*
- carbs $\to$ simple sugars (glucose)
- fats $\to$ fatty acids and glycerol
- proteins $\to$ amino acids

digestion begins at the mouth and different macronutrients are digested in different ways at different sites.

2 *absorption, and transportation of energy-yielding nutrients*
- absorbed through the intestinal lining
- healthy gut means more efficient absorption, enzymes, gut lining and microbiome all play key roles
- carried into the bloodstream or lymph system for delivery to tissues 
- sent to cells for energy use or storage

3 *production of metabolites*
- cells further break down nutrients through metabolic pathways (e.g. glycolysis, beta-oxidation)
- intermediate molecules (*metabolites*) like pyruvate and acetyl-CoA are produced
- the metabolites are the building blocks for ATP energy production
- the metabolites are then sent to the mitochondria of the cell and *ATP* is produced

now *glucose* can either be used for fuel, stored as glycogen or converted to fatty acids and stored in adipose tissue 

*more on glycogen*
- used literally everywhere
- *glucose* in storage form is *glycogen*
- glycogen stores will support **moderate** intensity exercise for 90 minutes or **high** intensity exercise for 30-45 minutes

at rest our body mainly prefers to use *fat*, as we increase the intensity of our exercise that changes to *carbs*

based on the type of training, the recommended carb range is different
