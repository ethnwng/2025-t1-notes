## anatomy of movement

we divide the body into 3 planes
- frontal: divides the body into *front and back* (front of face, back of head)
- horizontal: divides the body into *top and bottom* (like above the hips)
- sagittal: divides the body into *left and right* side

*joint angular movement/positions*
- there is *flexion, extension, or hyperextension*
- mostly movement in saggital plane
- arms, biceps, hands, feet, knees, those kinds of joints

- *abduction and adduction* movement away from the body like lifting arms outwards or inwords. 
- mainly movement in the frontal/coronal plane
- hips, neck, shoulder again

- *medial (internal) rotation/ lateral (external) rotation*
- turning left or right really
- mainly movement in the transverse/horizontal plane

- *pronation / supination*
- forearm movement
- pronation is turning to face the palm down
- supination is rotating to face palm up

- *inversion / eversion*
- like ankle joint movement
- inversion is like rotating big toe up first
- eversion is like little toe up first 

- *circumduction* 
- like moving the joint in a circular motion

**synovial joints**
all of the joints of our limbs *are synovial joints* and are designed for motion (unlike trunk or skull)

*joints are where bones kind of connect*. What does a synovial joint look like?

- there are two *articulating bones* the bones that the joint connects
- a *fibrous capsule* wraps the entire joint
- the *synovial cavity* is the small cavity between the two bones
- *synovial fluid* fils the synovial cavity
- *articular cartilage* is connective tissue that is on the articulating bones 

**factors for joint stability**
1. *the shape of the articulating bones*
	- hip joints have a ball and socket like connection
2. *connective tissue components - like ligaments , joint capsule*
	ex:
- lateral and medial collateral ligament
	- supports the knee
- ACL
	- prevents the tibia from sliding forward
	- typically injured when thigh rotates while lower leg is planted
- PCL
	- prevents tibia from sliding backwards 
3. *muscles and their tendons*
	- the rotator cuff muscles of the shoulder connect to some bones
4. *muscle contraction* 
	- the contraction can be used to stabilize joints
	- an *isometric* contraction generates force but its length remains the same because the force produced equals the external resistance
	- no movement at the joint
		- examples: holding a weight at a fixed position, pushing against a wall, holding a plank
	- an *isotonic* contraction is when the muscle contracts and changes length, moving a resistance
	- further divided into *concentric* the muscle shortens as it contracts (bicep curl up shortens) or *eccentric* the muscles lengthens as it contracts resisting a load (bicep curl back down)
5. *biarticular vs monoarticular muscles*
	- a biarticular muscle crosses *two joints* (rectus femoris, hamstrings)
	- monoarticular muscles cross just one joint (vastus muscles, glutes)
	- how muscles cross two joints affect how you can train them
		- eg the leg extension machine if you change how you sit and get muscles in a *longer position* you get different results
		- eg the preacher curl bicep curl vs incline curl
