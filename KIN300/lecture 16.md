### nervous system: functional organization
**central**
main components:
- brain
- spinal cord

there are alot of parts of the central nervous system:
- cerebrum (brain)
- diencephalon
- cerebellum
- brainstem
- spinalcord

**peripheral nervous**
- *spinal and peripheral nerves* supply structures in the trunk and limbs
- *cranial nerves* supply structures in the skull
	- responsible for carrying *motor* commands from the central nervous system (CNS) and *sensory* information from the periphery back to the CNS
- spinal cord is split into sections (cervical ,thoracic, lumbar, sacral)
- the cord is like protected by the vertebral column
	- *disc herniation:* when a vertebre presses on the spinal tissue, causes pain, numbness, and weakness
- each spinal cord segment is responsible for a different part of the body
- *dermatome:* the area of the skin supplied by a given spinal segment
- *myotome:* group of muscles supplied by a given spinal segment

- the spinal nerves form our peripheral nerves. 
	- nerves that supply the upper limb (arm) originate from the C5 to T1 spinal nerves
	- funny bone is a nerve that comes from there

**neurons**
- a *neuron* is cells of the nervous system and are in charge of information transfer
- a *nerve* is a bundle of axons 
the basic structure and function of a neuron
- *dendrites* (input) are tree-like fibers at the top they receive signals from other neurons and send them toward the cell body
- the *cell body* is the main part of the cell that ocntainst he nucleus
- the *axon* is the long cable like projection that carries the signal away from the cell body
- *nerve impulse* is the electrical signal that travels down the length of the axon

**motor areas of the brain**
4 main lobes:
- frontal: movement control, executive function (the boss) 
- temporal: hearing
- parietal: somatosensation
- occipital: vision

*where the each body part is controlled on the brain*
- looking at the frontal lobe taking slices there is a *frontal, primary,* and *posterior* motor cortex
the diagram used is called *homunculus* which shows where each part of the body is controlled in the **primary motor cortex**
*motor cortex:* neurons that control skeletal muscles
- size of representation of body is proportional to the degree of control
	- eg hand muscles vs leg muscles

*relationship between motor cortex and motor units*
- muscles with *small motor units* have a *large representation* in the motor cortex
- muscles with *large motor units* have a *small representation* in the motor cortex

**pathway from primary motor area to skeletal muscles**
- the *corticospinal tract* contains the axons of neurons that carry impulses from the *primary motor cortex* to skeletal muscle fibers
- almost a direct connection from the brain to the *alpha motor neurons* in the spinal cord, mostly for fine movements
- the nerves from the left and right side of the brain *cross at the decussation* point of the spinal cord, which is why our right side of the brain controls the left side of our body and vice versa

- we can use transcranial magnetic stimulation for studying this pathway in humans 
	- pretty much using some device send electrical signals to activate areas in the brain, then measure the response by sensors in a target muscle 

**premotor cortex area**
- they form a sort of *plan* which leads to *motor equivalence* what this means is that the *premotor area* has an *idea* of the types of signals needed to be sent and you can send that signal to any muscle group to replicate a movement
- also involved with *motor imagery*
	- babies that copy faces made to them
	- watching and learning something all takes part in the the premotor area
	- studies show that just thinking about doing an exercise has benefits

brainstem links the spinal cord to the brain
- controls breathing
- pain control
- other important stuff

**cerebellum**
- controls movement 
- has most of the brains neuerons
- receives extensive input from *sensory receptors*
- output to other brain areas that control movement
- important for posture & equilibrium; coordination and movement programs

function is exemplified by cerebellar lesions:
- *ataxia:* staggering or jerky movements,
	- deficit in posture
	- also affects distance perception
	- cant touch nose fast
