## guest speaker on cognition
- mainly *how to train* 

*action experiences* influence the *action prediction* (what we expect to happen next when someone is moving or something)
- novices show forward memory shift of ~40ms
- athletes show forward memory of ~160ms

we are focusing on *body reading* to get a prediction
- when we watch other people do something it activates the brain as if *we were the one doing something*

*mirror neuron* is the idea that the same neuron activates if you were doing an action or watching someone do an action

people who have *the specific motor experience* are better at the *perception* than people who've just seen an action performed a bunch
- pretty much people who can perform an action are better than people who just watch people perform the action

if we are *predicting* the next action
- those who are trained can predict things earlier than someone who is not trained

*in general* trained athletes are better at *predicting* the motor actions of a person

if you *interfere* with an athlete, make them use their muscles in some way, while they are watching someone their predicting ability *decreases* by alot
- making an athlete push against something while they watch 

**studies:**
- physically practicing a motion improves our *perception* of that same motion
- just watching a *motion* improves us a little bit
