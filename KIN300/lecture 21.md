### Exercise in Polluted and High Altitude Environments

air pollution recipe:
- gases (ozone, carbon monoxide, oxides of nitrogen, No2, No)
- particulates (solid or liquids, dust, soot, metal, pollen)

In canada:
- gas: there is the ozone which creates a heat layer
- particulates: wildfires causing soot

chronic affects:
- affects a lot of physical stuff
- causes 4.2 million deaths per year mostly in low/mid countries
- causes heart diseases
- bad for lungs etc

so air pollution is bad but exercise is good so we want to understand what happens if we want to *exercise* in a *polluted* area

*acute health affects of ozone*
- worsens lung function
- causes cough and dyspnoea
- more inflammatory markers

*acute health affects of particulates*
- inconsistent affect on lung function, exercise response, symptoms, and inflammatory response
- increases *blood pressure* 

*performance affects ozone*
- decrease in vo2 max 

*performance affects particulates*
- inconsistent performance affects 
- possible technical effects in skill based sports

**recommendations for working out in a polluted area**
- try to not exercise in polluted environments
things you should do
- *monitor* air pollution levels
- *move* away from pollution
- *schedule* a time where pollution is low: more pollution as day goes on
- *shorter* exercise times in polluted areas is better, intensity doesnt matter (*eat more supplements*)

**altitude**
ams - mountain sickness
hape - high altitude pulmonary oedema

prevention:
- rapidly getting up to altitude is a risk factor
	- should aclimatise to the altitude
	- blood becomes more efficient carries more O2
	- makes more red blood cells
- stay hydrated
	- easier to be dehydrated in high altitude
	- eat antioxidants
- medication 
	- most of the time they are banned substances
	- medications that increase blood vessels to promote oxygen rate
- keep high iron levels since we need those for blood cells
