muscle contraction features
- speed
- tension/force output (strength)
- endurance capacity (muscular endurance)

each have their own mechanism
- speed : the type of myosin protein -> myosin ATphase
- tension : number of cross bridges we can make (related to size)
- endurance capacity : metabolic pathway: (aerobic, anaerobic)


contraction cycle
- release calcium from the sarcolemma
- calcium binds to the troponin and creates the the cross-bridge reaction(myosin-actin binding)

*how to increase muscle tension?*
- the number of cross bridges is directly related to the tension.
three main factors:
*size factor:* muscle fiber diameter (dependent on fiber type); the larger the diameter the more filaments and therefore more cross bridges
*mechanical factor:* the amount of overlap between thick and thin filaments -> length of muscle
*neural factor:* the amount of calcium released into the sarcoplasm -> depends on *neural stimulation*

**length-tension relationship**
- the longer the muscle is at rest the more tension it is able to produce

**neural input to skeletal muscle**
- the brain sends a *motor neuron* which transmits impulses from the nervous system to the muscle fiber

the *neuromuscular junction* is the link between the motor neuron and the skeletal muscle fibers


the release of calcium starts from the *nervous system*
- *motor neuron* from brain travels through the spinal cord and on the *neural muscular junction*
- the *motor neuron* triggers the release of ACh in the *neuromuscular junction* the area before the muscle tissue
- the *ACh* goes to the sarcolemma which when electrically excited by the ACh, causes the release of calcium

-> botox blocks the ability for the ACh to go to the sarcolemma effectively paralyzing the muscle since the calcium would never be released


**motor units**
*defn* a **motor unit** is $1$ motor neuron and all the muscle fibers it innervates each motor unit is responsible for a given set of muscle fibers

during a single *muscle twitch* 
- theres a latent, contraction, and relaxation period
	- latent: point up to the motor unit arriving at the NMJ
	- contraction: when $Ca^{2+}$ is being released
	- resting: when the $Ca^{2+}$ has stopped being released and the fibers are going back to baseline

two methods to *increase tension (neurally):*
1. increase frequency of stimulation
	- send more signals
	- as we send more signals the muscle twitches *build up* and increases the tension
2. recruit more motor units (recruit more muscle fibers)
	- get more muscle fibers
	- normally recruit gradually depending on how much power is needed over time

the size of motor units is related to the size of the muscle fiber
- smaller motor units control *fine and delicate* movemnts (hands, mouth, eyeballs)
- large motor units like quads, back, chest
- normally recruitment goes by size so start with small twitch fibers then IIA and IIB/x

*effects:*
increase in motor units usage increase the calories burned and therefore is good for weight loss
