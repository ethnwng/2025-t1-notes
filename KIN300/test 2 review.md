**length tension relationship**
the amount of force a muscle can generate is related to the number of cross bridges/opportunity to make cross-bridges

so talking about length of a muscle

the *isometric (static) length* of a muscle has a certain amount of potential to make crossbridges
- i.e there is an optimal length for a muscle which generates the most amount of force
- e.g if you roll wrist outwards or inwards you cant generate as much force in your grip strength compared to if you had your wrist in a more neutral position
	- this is because the potential to make cross bridges is mechanically blocked in both ways 
	- if you roll downwards the muscle is kind of clumped up (makes it hard for it to contract)
	- if you roll outwards the muscle is fully stretched (the contraction needs to pull harder)

**the effect of firing frequency**
each muscle twitch has 3 phases:
1. muscle before neuron reaches nmj
2. after the neuron reaches and Ca is released causing cross bridges
3. after the cross-bridges release and the muscle relaxes

main idea: if you keep zapping the muscle (sending more and more neurons) before the 3rd phase has ended you get two overlapping twitches almost, and therefore more tension .
- i.e you keep sending neurons which releases more Calcium before the muscle relaxes making it so that the tension increases

**angular movements**
there are three planes 
1. frontal (front and back)
2. horizontal/transverse (upper and lower)
3. saggital plane (left and right)

the frontal deals with abduction (away from body) or adduction (towards body) movements

the horizontal deals with rotation (medial-internal/lateral-external)

the saggital deals with flexion and extension
