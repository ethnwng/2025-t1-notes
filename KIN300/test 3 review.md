**pulmonary physiology**
the controls of the pulmonary system is based on a bunch of *physics* laws	

*boyles law:* The *pressure* from a gas is *related* to the *volume* of the space of the container (smaller container = more pressure and vice versa)
- when we take a breath in *we create more space in our lungs* **lowering** the pressure in our thoracic cavity meaning *air* will flow into our lungs
	- *note:* gas will always look to flow to *lower* areas of *pressure*
- when we breathe out we increase the pressure in our thoracic cavity making pressure flow out

*daltons law:* the total pressure (proportion of total pressure attributed to a gas) is the sum of all partial pressures

*oxygen transport:* when oxygen is transported from different levels of the body the *partial pressure* of the oxygen changes based on a bunch of different stuff.
- pretty much the partial pressure of oxygen (in air) steps down depending on where it is in the body.

at lower *partial pressures* it is easier for oxygen to *get off* (?) of the hemoglobin
- the more the muscles are using oxygen (taking o2 out of the blood) it lowers the partial pressure in the blood further and makes it easier to keep taking oxygen off the blood

the *arterial partial pressure* of oxygen is **constant** in the body no matter the intensity of exercise going on in the body.
